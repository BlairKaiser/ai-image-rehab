How it works

?What the ripple is

A picture from an image generator carries a trace of two kinds. The eye reads both as "dirty" and "this is AI", most of all from afar.

→The order of work

  1. Open a picture. By file, by dropping it, or from the clipboard. The source file is never changed.
  2. Press “Remove ripple”. If the picture has soft, painted shading, choose “Soft shading” first. It takes from a few seconds to half a minute.
  3. Look. The mouse wheel zooms. The “Before” button (or the key B), while held, shows the picture as it came. Check both large and small: a drawn mistake is seen better from afar.
  4. Fix spots by hand, if any are left. Choose what to do, select the spot, press the button under the selection. Not happy: Ctrl+Z, the selection comes back.
  5. Download. “Download PNG” hands the file to the browser, “Save to folder” puts it into a folder beside the program and shows the path.

2How the auto clean works

  • It splits the picture into calm fills and edges.
  • In a fill it compares every pixel with the mean colour of its own fill nearby. A deviation under 5 brightness levels of 255 counts as dirt and is evened out. Over 10 counts as drawing (a shadow, blush, a highlight, a freckle) and is left alone.
  • At a line it looks for a strip: lighter than the fill beside a dark line, or darker beside a light shape. It pulls the strip to the colour of the fill, by no more than 14 % of the line's contrast.
  • No shape moves: lines, shadows, highlights and small details stay where they are.

The three numbers “before → after” show how much of this trace the picture has: how far the strip beside lines is from the fill (the worst tenth of the edges is taken), how uneven the fills are near lines, and how much fine grain there is. All in brightness levels of 255. Compare “before” and “after” of one picture; different pictures cannot be compared by these numbers, a painted one reads higher because of its own shading.

3How fixing by hand works

You select a spot and choose one of four ways. Only what is under the selection changes; the rest of the picture stays pixel for pixel.

  • Remove a mark. A network (LaMa, trained on anime and manga) cuts the selection out and draws the spot anew from what is around it: it carries on the lines and fills that reach the selection. It is strong on small things: a stroke, a speck, a smear, a blot. Select the mark itself, whole, with its ends, and nothing else: everything under the selection is drawn its own way, lashes and outlines too. It runs on the processor, one to three seconds. The “Plain fill” switch does the same without a network: it paints the selection over with one fill.
  • This way can be carried out in up to four manners. “Fast”: this network alone, and its plain fills are replaced by the exact colours of the picture itself, so there is neither a colour shift nor grain. “Refined”: after it a big anime model from this computer (Illustrious) draws the spot clean; lines come out crisper where there is a lot of small detail. “From scratch”: the big model draws the spot anew with a guide trained to fill holes. “Plain fill”: no networks, one neighbouring fill. The big model takes 20 seconds or more and uses the video card; the “Another take” button draws the same spot differently.
  • Rim along lines. No network, and exact. Dark lines and all neighbouring shapes with their soft edges stay under the selection; the rest is painted over with the neighbouring fill. Select generously, right over the lashes.
  • Stray shadow, gap. Only dark lines stay; everything else in the selection becomes one fill: the one that borders the selection most, or the one you clicked with the eyedropper. The colour flows in from the edges of that fill, so its shading carries on.
  • Draw a line. It removes nothing: it draws. Where you brushed, a line appears in the colour of the nearest outline. A click with the eyedropper on a neighbouring line takes its colour and sets the brush to its width. The eyedropper can take any fill as well: then the brush simply paints with it.

Why there are several ways: a thin tip of a lash and a stray stroke are built alike, and neither a rule nor a network knows which of them was meant. With a wide selection the network redraws the lashes too; the exact ways will not touch them, but will not erase a stray stroke growing out of a lash either.

All measures are tuned for pictures about 1300 pixels across and grow with the picture. The wand finds a thin mark under the click by itself; a thin tip of a lash is a mark for it too, so look at what got selected and take the extra away with the brush set to “−”.

So that nothing gets spoiled: every step can be undone, and the selection comes back with it; the “Before” button shows the picture as it came; the work is written to disk after every step and taken up again when the page is started; the source file is never changed.

!What the program cannot do

It invents nothing new: it will not add a lash or mend a finger or a shape. It removes what is extra and draws a line where you drew it. The network draws what is likely, not what is true: look at every result. The picture is sent nowhere: everything is computed on this computer.
